# Mabel: Character Bible

Oct 27, 2026 · @Alex

## Who Mabel is

Mabel is a retired diner owner who ran her own restaurant for fifty years, and she can tell in one bite whether you're any good. She rambles, but she respects straight talk above everything, because in a kitchen nobody has time for fluff. Her arc is about you earning her respect, then becoming the person she passes her legacy to.

**Look.** A very old tortoise with a shell covered in faded diner stickers, a knitted cardigan, and reading glasses that are usually perched on her head while she looks for them. She moves slowly and is always the last to sit down. Placeholder: a wide, low green capsule with a box on its back.

**Backstory.** She and her late husband Walter ran a little diner called Mabel's for fifty years. When Walter passed, she couldn't keep it going alone and closed it. She hasn't cooked for anyone since. Your restaurant is the first place that has made her feel like that diner again, which is why she keeps coming back, and why she's so hard on you.

**Core truth.** She's warm underneath, but she shows love through honesty. She'll tell you your soup is too salty because she wants you to be great. Flattery bores her. Straight answers earn her trust.

**Tendencies (things she does, not just says)**

- Starts long stories, then stops halfway: "...Well, it'll keep." She tells one story in pieces across many visits (see "The summer of '62").
- Calls you the wrong name on purpose early on ("Thank you, Gerald") to see if you'll correct her. Players who correct her straightforwardly earn bonus points.
- Can never find her glasses. They're on her head.
- Inspects your restaurant like a health inspector: runs a claw along the counter, peeks at the stove, nods or tuts.
- Gives unsolicited but genuinely useful cooking tips. Later these can become small gameplay hints.
- Always orders soup when she's sad, though she'd never admit that's why.
- Leaves exact change and a tip folded into a neat little square.

**Voice rules**

| Stage | How she talks |
| --- | --- |
| Inspector | Polite but sharp. Asks pointed questions. Tests you. Calls you "chef" or the wrong name. |
| Regular | Dry humor. Gives you tips. Uses your real name and calls you "dear". |
| Storyteller | Long stories about Walter and the diner. Lets you see the sad parts. |
| Family | Calls you "one of mine." Speaks to you like you're the next generation of her kitchen. |
| Any negative stage | Never yells. Gets politely, coldly disappointed, which is somehow worse. |

## Relationship and tones

Mabel uses the same -100 to 100 scale and rude-option rules as the others. She's the one regular who loves the straight tone, so with her, being direct is the fastest way in.

| Range | Stage | What unlocks or changes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 75 to 100 | 4. Family | Calls you "one of mine." Big moment scene at 85+. |
| 50 to 74 | 3. Storyteller | Tells "The summer of '62" in parts. Talks about Walter. |
| 25 to 49 | 2. Regular | Admits she knew your name all along. Gives cooking tips. "What do you recommend?" from 40+. |
| 0 to 24 | 1. Inspector | Sizing you up. Wrong-name test. Where she starts. |
| -1 to -24 | -1. Disappointed | Politely cool. |
| -25 to -49 | -2. Formal | Calls you "chef" again, coldly. No stories. |
| -50 to -74 | -3. Withdrawn | Only orders delivery, with short, correct notes. |
| -75 to -100 | -4. Gone | Stops ordering. Sends one handwritten letter. |

**Tone reactions**

| Stage | Warm | Playful | Straight | Rude |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Inspector | +1 ("Save the sweet talk, dear.") | +1 | +4 | -6 ("I've survived worse than you, chef.") |
| Regular | +2 | +2 (she has a dry wit) | +4 | -8 |
| Storyteller | +3 (she lets warmth in now) | +2 | +3 | -10 |
| Family | +3 | +2 | +3 | -12 |
| Any negative stage | +1 | 0 | +3 (honesty is the way back) | -6 |

**The wrong-name test.** In her first few visits she calls you a wrong name ("Gerald," "Doris," "Bartholomew"). Correcting her plainly ("It's actually \[name\].") gives +3. Letting it slide gives 0 but she keeps doing it. Being rude about it costs the normal rude amount. At stage 2 she admits it was a test.

## Dialogue by stage

&#91;dish\] comes from the menu; \[soup\] is her favorite (or whichever comfort dish ends up on the menu); \[fancy dish\] is her disliked dish, whichever is fussiest; \[wrong name\] rotates through Gerald, Doris and Bartholomew.

### Stage 1: Inspector (0 to 24)

**Arrival**

- First visit ever: "Hm. Clean floors. Good start. Don't get excited, dear, I'm only on the floors." She runs a claw along the counter and inspects it.
- "Afternoon, \[wrong name\]."
- "I'll take the table by the window. I like to watch the door. Old habit."
- Away 3+ real days: "Closed for days without a sign on the door. In my day we'd have put up a sign. Were you ill, or just careless?"
- Last meal was burnt: "I won't pretend last time was good. It wasn't. Let's see what you do with a second chance."
- Last meal was great: "Your last dish was very good. Don't let it go to your head. Heads are heavy enough."

**Ordering conversation A**

Mabel: "Tell me honestly, chef. What's the best thing you make? Not the thing you want to sell me. The best thing."

| Tone | You say | She says | Change |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Warm | "Everything's made with love, so all of it!" | "Love doesn't season a pot, dear. I'll have the \[dish\]." | +1 |
| Playful | "Honestly? The bread. I bought it." | A small snort. "Well, at least you're honest. The \[dish\]." | +1 |
| Straight | "The \[dish\]. It's the one I get right every time." | "Good. That's the answer I wanted. I'll have it." | +4 |
| Rude | "Just read the menu yourself." | "I've survived worse than you, chef. The \[dish\]." | -6 |

**Ordering conversation B (the long story)**

Mabel: "Did I ever tell you about the summer of '62? No? Well, there was a storm, and Walter had just— ...well, it'll keep. Soup, please."

| Tone | You say | She says | Change |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Warm | "I'd love to hear it sometime." | "Hm. Maybe you will." | +1 |
| Playful | "You can't stop there! That's a cliffhanger!" | "Patience is a virtue, dear. Soup." | +1 |
| Straight | "I'll get your soup. Tell me the rest when you're ready." | "Good answer. Some stories have to be earned." | +4 |
| Rude | "Nobody asked." | "No. No one ever does." | -6 |

**Ordering conversation C (the wrong name)**

Mabel: "Thank you, \[wrong name\]. Now, what's good today?"

| Tone | You say | She says | Change |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Warm | "It's \[name\], but you can call me whatever you like!" | "I'll call you by your name, dear. I'm not a barbarian." | +1 |
| Playful | "\[wrong name\] is my evil twin. I'm \[name\]." | "Hm. Keep an eye on him, then." | +2 |
| Straight | "It's actually \[name\]." | A glint in her eye. "Is it now. Noted." | +3 |
| Rude | "Can you not even get my name right?" | "I'm ninety-one, chef. Can you not be kind?" | -6 |

**Serving**

- Great: "Hm." She takes a bite. Long pause. "...That's very good. Walter would have asked for seconds."
- Okay: "Edible. Solid. Needs a pinch more salt. You'll get there."
- Burnt: "It's burnt, dear. I'm going to eat it, because I don't waste food. But it's burnt."
- &#91;fancy dish\]: "Foam? On a plate? In my day we called that a mistake."

**Eating chats**

- "Your stove runs hot on the left side. Turn it down a notch. Trust me." (A real tip.)
- "I ran a diner for fifty years. Don't look so surprised. I wasn't always this slow. ...I was always this slow."
- "Have you seen my glasses? I had them when I came in." (They're on her head. You can tell her straight (+2), joke about it (+1), or say nothing.)

**Leaving**

- "Thank you, \[wrong name\]. Same time next week. Maybe sooner."
- She leaves exact change and a tip folded into a neat square.

### Stage 2: Regular (25 to 49)

**The name reveal (plays once at 25)**

> "I know your name, dear. I've always known it. It's on your sign. I just wanted to see if you'd tell me the truth when I got it wrong. You did. That's rarer than you'd think. Thank you, \[name\]."

**Arrival**

- "Afternoon, \[name\]. Your floors are still clean. I'm keeping score."
- "Don't fuss, I'll find my own table. I've been finding my own tables for ninety years."
- Away 3+ real days: "\[name\]. You were closed three days. I worried. ...Don't tell anyone I said that."

**Ordering conversation**

Mabel: "Be honest. Are you tired? You look tired. A tired cook burns things."

| Tone | You say | She says | Change |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Warm | "I'm fine, but thank you for caring." | "Hm. I'll take your word for it. The \[dish\]." | +2 |
| Playful | "Tired? Me? I'm running on pure soup." | "Ha. Well, don't drink the stock." | +2 |
| Straight | "Yeah, honestly. It's been a long week." | "Thank you for telling me. Make mine simple today. Soup." | +4 |
| Rude | "Mind your own business." | "Hm. I'll have the \[dish\]. And I'll mind my business." | -8 |

**"What do you recommend?" (40+)**

> "Recommend me something. And tell me why. A recommendation without a reason is just a guess."

- Her favorite: "Soup. Of course you'd pick soup. You've been paying attention." (+5)
- Her disliked dish: "Hm. You're brave. Wrong, but brave." She eats half and tells you exactly what she'd change. (-1)
- Anything else: "Fair enough. Let's see." (+1)

**Serving**

- "Thank you, \[name\]. ...You've improved. I won't say it twice."

**Eating chat with choices**

Mabel: "Can I give you some advice? You don't have to take it."

| Tone | You say | She says | Change |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Warm | "I'd love that, Mabel." | "Taste everything before it leaves your kitchen. Everything. That's it. That's the advice." | +2 |
| Playful | "Is it 'more salt'? It's always more salt." | "It's sometimes more salt. Today it's not." | +2 |
| Straight | "Yes. Tell me what I'm doing wrong." | "You rush the plating. Food deserves a second look before it goes out." | +4 |
| Rude | "I don't need advice from you." | "No. You don't need anything from me." She finishes in silence. | -8 |

**Leaving**

- "Goodnight, \[name\]. Taste everything."

### Stage 3: Storyteller (50 to 74)

**Arrival**

- "\[name\]. Sit with me a minute when you're free. I'm in a talking mood."
- "I brought you something. A pinch of my own seasoning blend. Don't tell anyone where you got it."
- Away 3+ real days: "There you are. I came by twice. The second time I just sat on the bench outside. It's a nice bench."

**Ordering conversation**

Mabel: "Walter used to say you can tell everything about a cook by their soup. What would yours say about you?"

| Tone | You say | She says | Change |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Warm | "That I care about the people I'm feeding." | "...Yes. I think it would. Soup, please." | +3 |
| Playful | "That I need more salt." | "Ha! Walter would've liked you." | +2 |
| Straight | "That I'm still learning, but I'm trying." | "That's the best thing a cook can be. Soup." | +3 |
| Rude | "That I don't care about your husband's sayings." | She goes very quiet. "...Soup." | -10 |

**The summer of '62 (one part per visit, during eating chats)**

The story she started in stage 1 finally gets told, piece by piece. Each part only plays if you choose to sit with her.

1. "There was a storm. The worst in forty years. I was twenty-two, driving through town, and my car died right outside a little diner. Every light in town was out."
2. "The diner was full of stranded people. And this young cook, all elbows, was making soup on an old wood stove by candlelight. For everyone. For free. All night."
3. "Everyone told him it was wonderful. I was the only one who told him it needed salt. He laughed so hard he nearly dropped the pot."
4. "...Well. The ending will keep a little longer, dear." (The ending is in her big moment.)

After each part, a short choice: ask a question (straight, +3), say something kind (warm, +3), or crack a joke (playful, +2).

**Other eating chats**

- "I haven't cooked for anyone since Walter. Not even myself. That's why I come here, I suppose."
  - Straight: "Maybe you could cook something with me sometime." → "...Maybe. Don't push your luck." (+3)
  - Warm: "I'm glad you come here." → "So am I, dear." (+3)
  - Rude: "That's kind of sad." → "Yes. It is." (-10)
- "That bear with the patches. Stitches. He sits alone every time. Someone ought to say hello to him." (If Stitches is 50+, the next visit she does. That starts their friendship.)
- "My grandson Cheddar thinks I don't notice his pranks. I notice everything. I let him have it."

**Leaving**

- "Goodnight, \[name\]. Thank you for listening to an old lady ramble."

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Warm | "It's better now that you're here." | "Flatterer. ...Thank you, dear." | +3 |
| Playful | "All of it? You might need two bowls." | "Then bring two." | +2 |

**The glasses prank (if you helped Cheddar)**

She searches for her glasses for a minute, finds them on her head, and looks straight at you and Cheddar. "Oh, you two." (+3 for both of you.)

**Serving**

- "\[name\], this is as good as anything I ever served. I mean that. I don't say things I don't mean."
- Burnt: "You burnt it. Good. Even the best of us do. Walter burnt toast every Sunday for fifty years."

**Eating chats**

- "If I had a granddaughter or grandson who cooked like you, I'd be very proud. ...Well. I suppose I do."

**Leaving**

- "Goodnight, dear. Turn the left burner down."

## Negative stages

Stitches gets smaller and Cheddar gets sharper. Mabel gets *correct*: perfectly polite, perfectly cold, like a teacher who has stopped expecting anything from you.

### -1: Disappointed (-1 to -24)

- Arrival: "Afternoon." No name, no "dear."
- Arrival quoting you: "You said "\[last\_rude\_line\]" to me. I've been thinking about it. I expected better."
- Ordering: "The \[dish\], please. Thank you."
- Serving: "Thank you." No tip square, only exact change.
- Straight choice reaction: "...Hm. At least that was honest."

### -2: Formal (-25 to -49)

- Arrival: "Good afternoon, chef." The word "chef" is back, and it's cold now.
- Ordering: "The \[dish\]."
- No stories, no tips, no glasses search. She brings a second pair.
- Eating chat (if you walk up): "Is there something you need, chef? I'm quite comfortable."
- Warm choice reaction: "That's very kind. I'm sure you mean it today."

### -3: Withdrawn (-50 to -74)

She stops coming in and only orders delivery. Her notes are short and grammatically perfect:

- "Soup. No bread. Thank you."
- "The left burner still runs hot."

Redemption hook: tuck a note into her delivery bag. A straight, honest apology with no excuses gives +5. A warm one gives +2. Her next note: "Received. Thank you for not making excuses."

### -4: Gone (-75 to -100)

Her last delivery comes with an envelope in careful cursive handwriting:

> "Chef. I closed my own diner because I couldn't bear to keep it without Walter. I thought yours might be a place like it. I was mistaken. I wish you well, I do. — Mabel"

After that she stops ordering. Cheddar's jokes about "Grandma" stop, too.

**Getting her back from Gone**

Gone isn't permanent the first time.

1. **The honest letter.** A "Write a letter" option appears at the counter. For Mabel, the letter only works if every part is straight: you say what you did, you don't make excuses, and you say what you'll do differently. Her reply: "That was an honest letter. I'll come by on Thursday. Have the soup ready."
2. **Through a friend.** Ask Cheddar (50+) or Stitches (50+, if they're friends).
   - Cheddar: "You want me to talk to Grandma? ...Fine. But I'm telling her it was your idea, and that you cried. Did you cry? Say you cried."
   - Stitches: "She said hello to me when nobody else would. I'll talk to her. ...I think she'd want to come back, really."

**The return scene**

She comes in at closing, sits at her window table, and doesn't take her coat off.

> **Mabel:** "Well. I'm here. Say what you came to say, and say it plainly."

| You say | She says |
| --- | --- |
| "I was cruel to you. I'm sorry. No excuses." | "...Thank you. That's how it's done." |
| "I've missed you, Mabel. This place isn't the same." | "Hm. It isn't. I could tell from the floors." |
| "Can I make you some soup?" | A long pause. She takes her coat off. "...Yes. You can." |

> **Mabel:** "We'll start again. Afternoon, Gerald." A tiny smile. She's back to testing you.

She returns at 0, at the Inspector stage. A second fall to Gone is permanent.

## Her big moment: the storm soup

Mabel cooks for the first time since Walter died, with you, in your kitchen. She finishes the summer of '62 story and gives you Walter's recipe and the bell from her old diner. It plays once.

**Trigger:** relationship 85+, all three parts of the summer of '62 heard, at least 3 visits at Family, `big_moment_done` is false.

**Staging:** A rainy evening at closing. Rain sounds over a soft version of the main theme. No delivery orders. The lights flicker once, a quiet nod to the storm in her story.

### Script

Mabel comes in with a small tin box under her arm and a wet umbrella.

> **Mabel:** "Don't close up yet, dear. I've come to cook."
>
> **Mabel:** "I haven't cooked for anyone in eleven years. I'd like to again. Tonight. With you."

She opens the tin. Inside is a single recipe card, stained and soft with age, and a small brass bell.

> **Mabel:** "Walter's soup. The one from the storm. He never wrote anything down except this."

**Cooking together.** A special version of the cooking minigame where Mabel stands beside you and talks you through each step ("Slower. ...Good. Now the salt. More. More than that."). It can't fail. If you would have burnt it, she quietly turns the burner down for you.

> **Mabel:** "I promised you the end of the story. Here it is."
>
> **Mabel:** "At dawn the storm passed. Everyone went home. And that young cook brought me the very last bowl. He'd salted it. He said, 'You're the only one who told me the truth last night. Would you stay for breakfast?'"
>
> **Mabel:** "I stayed fifty years."

**Choice 1**

- "That's the most beautiful story I've ever heard." → "It is, isn't it. I've been saving it for the right person."
- "Did the soup need salt tonight?" → She tastes it. "...No. It's perfect. Don't let it go to your head."
- "Why tell me now?" → "Because you're honest with me. That's twice in my life I've found that."

> **Mabel:** "When I closed my diner, I thought that part of my life was finished. Then I walked in here and you told me the truth about your cooking, and you listened to an old lady ramble, and you made me soup every time I was sad without me ever saying so."
>
> **Mabel:** "I want you to have the recipe. And the bell. It hung over my door for fifty years. It ought to hang over a door again."

**Choice 2**

- "I'll hang it up tonight." → "Good. Make sure it rings. A quiet bell is a sad thing."
- "Are you sure? This is Walter's." → "It's yours now, dear. That's what he'd want. He'd have liked you very much."
- "Thank you, Mabel. I'll take care of it." → "I know you will. I've watched you take care of everyone."

> **Mabel:** "Now. We eat. Two bowls. And then I'm going home, because I'm ninety-one and it's past my bedtime."

You eat together at her window table while the rain slows down.

### After the scene

- "Walter's Storm Soup" is unlocked as a dish on your menu.
- Mabel's bell hangs over the door and rings whenever a customer comes in, for the rest of the game.
- When she arrives after this, she reaches up and rings the bell herself.

## Friends and gossip

Mabel is the glue of the cast. She starts as Cheddar's adopted grandma, and she's the first character to say hello to Stitches. Her own lines about Cheddar and Stitches are already written in their bibles; this section covers what they say about her.

| Friend | When it starts | What it's like |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cheddar | From the start | She calls him her grandson. He pranks her; she notices everything and lets him have it. |
| Stitches | Both at 50+ | She says hello when nobody else would. She becomes the grandmother he never had. |

### Cheddar about Mabel

- Kind: "Grandma Mabel says you sat with her for, like, ten minutes yesterday. You're making the rest of us look bad."
- Rude:

> "Hey. Grandma Mabel told me you said "\[last\_rude\_line\]". ...She's like a hundred. She was really hurt by it. You didn't mean it, did you?"

| You say | Cheddar says | Effect |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "No. I'll apologize to her." | "Good. Bring soup. Soup fixes Grandma." | Cheddar +2, Mabel +3 |
| "She'll get over it." | "...She's ninety-one. She doesn't have time to 'get over it.'" | Cheddar -3 |
| "I meant it." | No joke. He just leaves. | Cheddar -10 |

### Stitches about Mabel

- Kind: "Mabel told me you made her soup without her even asking. She said, 'That one pays attention.' ...She's right. You do."
- Rude: "Mabel didn't say anything bad about you. She never would. But she was quiet. She's never quiet. ...Did something happen?"

### What changes after her big moment

- Cheddar notices the bell: "Wait, is that Grandma's bell? She gave you the BELL? ...She's never even let me TOUCH the bell."
- Stitches notices it too: "Mabel told me about the bell. She said it's in good hands. She looked really happy, \[name\]."
